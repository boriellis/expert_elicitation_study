Seabird Flight Heights Evidence Dossier

Background

The flight heights of seabirds are becoming increasingly relevant as offshore wind energy development expands globally. Flight height is a key input to collision risk models, however, data remain sparse and largely anecdotal for many species. Where data does exist, it is frequently reported in inconsistent formats that are difficult to synthesize.

For this portion of the elicitation workshop, please consider the following scenario:

Our team is estimating seabird vulnerability to collision with offshore wind energy facilities planned off the northeastern continental United States. To inform this effort, we’ve compiled flight height data for seabirds occurring in the North Atlantic by collating results from two vessel-based survey studies: one conducted in the North Sea off the coast of the Netherlands (Leopold et al., 2004) and one in Rhode Island Sound (Paton et al., 2010). In both studies, observed flight heights were recorded in discrete altitudinal bands above sea level. The percentage of individuals observed in each band is used as a proxy for the percentage of time a species spends flying within that band. Our metric of interest is the zone between 25 and 125 m above sea level, as this range overlaps the rotor-swept zone of the planned offshore wind turbines and is therefore relevant to collision sensitivity.

Six seabird species of interest were not observed in sufficient numbers in these surveys for flight height data to be reported. We are therefore asking for your estimates of the percentage of time these species spend flying between 25 and 125 m above sea level, based on your understanding of their biology and flight behavior. To ensure a common baseline of information between expert respondents, we have compiled the most relevant available information on flight heights for these species, or for closely related species when species-specific data were unavailable. Please review this background information before providing your responses. Note that as offshore wind turbine size differs between facilities, the flight heights are reported in a number of different formats. We summarize the results here using the same formats are the original sources, including for rotor-swept zones with 20, 25, and 30m air gaps (minimum height of the turbine blades above the sea), and estimates of mean flight height.

We recognize that some participants may already be familiar with the results of the reference study. This does not preclude participation; however, to reduce the influence of potential past exposure to the blinded data on your responses, please refrain from direct review of the reference datasets we’re using for this portion of the study directly when reviewing this dossier and providing your survey responses.
